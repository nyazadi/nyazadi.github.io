about

I came to software from the science side. I studied bioengineering and bioinformatics at UC San Diego, worked around pipelines and data, and found the tooling more interesting than the science it served.

Reliability isn't a feature you add at the end. It's a property of how the thing was built.

What I care about

Whether a system actually holds up. Not whether it demos well, but whether it behaves on a bad day, with real data moving through it and nobody watching. That's the engineering I want to be doing.

Quality gets treated like a stage at the end of a project. That's backwards. Build the pipeline well and make the feedback fast, and quality follows. Testing is how you find out what you actually built, so find out early and often.

The work has to land somewhere real. I'm on diagnostics platforms, so what comes out of a pipeline becomes an answer for a patient. That's why I take reliability seriously.

And the engineers on the other end of my tools matter. A tool that's technically correct and confusing is a tool nobody opens. I ship smaller and make sure it gets used.

What I use

Python and TypeScript, day to day. AWS, Kafka and CI/CD on the infrastructure side. React and React Native when I'm building something people touch. Lately a lot of LLM tooling and MCP, plus Git, SQL, Jira and Figma.
